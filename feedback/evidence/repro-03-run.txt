6. Conclusion
================================================================================================
Observed: ONE externally-owned account, 0xd58596620Ee6951B2958a931949e4ff7ce6222be,
is the owner of all 5 listed contracts (OperatorPermissionsRegistry, MarginBank, PerpPool (ETH), PerpPoolFactory, PerpPool beacon), and of the liquidation engine and insurance fund as well.
It is a plain EOA (no code), not a multisig or timelock; the same address is also the bank's fee recipient (read from getSystemConfig).
In the section 4 simulations this owner is accepted as upgrade authority on every contract tested, and a neutral address is not.
Ownership transfer is two-step (pendingOwner + acceptOwnership present) on: registry, bank, pool(ETH), factory; not on: beacon.

Context: a single-EOA admin is normal and expected on a testnet, and this is not a vulnerability here. The note is for mainnet readiness: if this key, or
this ownership layout, were carried to mainnet unchanged, one key compromise or mistake could upgrade the registry (and with it change which operators are
authorized), the collateral-holding bank, the factory and every beacon-proxied pool. Suggested before mainnet: a multisig (optionally behind a timelock) as
owner of all of them, and documenting who holds upgrade authority.
